# Overseas AI Policy and Regulation Sources

This file defines the preferred official sources for tracking overseas AI policy and regulation. Use official regulatory and government sources before media analysis.

## Source Priority

- P0: Government websites, regulator announcements, official legal texts, official guidance, standards bodies.
- P1: International organizations and official policy observatories.
- P2: Regulator speeches, consultation papers, official FAQs, implementation guidance.
- P3: Reuters, Financial Times, Wall Street Journal, Bloomberg, and reputable law firm analysis as secondary interpretation.
- P4: Newsletters, social media, and opinion pieces only as discovery signals.

## United States

Primary sources:

- White House
- Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP)
- NIST AI Risk Management Framework and AI standards pages
- U.S. AI Safety Institute / Center for AI Standards and Innovation where applicable
- Federal Trade Commission (FTC)
- Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)
- U.S. Copyright Office
- Federal Register
- Department of Commerce
- Department of Energy where AI infrastructure and data centers are relevant

Focus topics:

- Executive actions and federal AI policy
- AI safety, model evaluation, and standards
- Consumer protection, deceptive AI claims, AI advertising
- Public company AI disclosure and AI washing
- Copyright, training data, generated content, authorship
- Data centers, chips, export controls, cybersecurity

## European Union

Primary sources:

- European Commission: Shaping Europe's Digital Future
- EU AI Office
- EUR-Lex
- European Parliament
- Council of the European Union
- European Data Protection Board (EDPB)
- European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS)

Focus topics:

- EU AI Act implementation
- General-purpose AI model rules
- Codes of Practice and compliance timelines
- Model transparency, safety, and evaluation obligations
- GDPR and AI data governance
- Copyright, data access, and platform accountability

## United Kingdom

Primary sources:

- Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT)
- UK AI Security Institute
- Information Commissioner's Office (ICO)
- Competition and Markets Authority (CMA)
- GOV.UK policy papers and consultation documents

Focus topics:

- Frontier model safety and evaluation
- AI regulation principles and sectoral regulation
- Data protection, automated decisions, and privacy
- Foundation model competition and market structure
- AI adoption and national AI strategy

## International Organizations and Standards Bodies

Primary sources:

- OECD.AI
- G7 / Hiroshima AI Process
- Global Partnership on AI (GPAI)
- United Nations
- UNESCO
- International Telecommunication Union (ITU)
- ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42
- World Economic Forum only as secondary context

Focus topics:

- Cross-border AI governance
- AI safety principles and model evaluation cooperation
- AI ethics, education, and human rights
- Technical standards and risk management
- International policy convergence and divergence

## Reporting Rules

For each policy item, capture:

1. What changed?
2. Who is affected?
3. What is the timeline?
4. What are the compliance implications?
5. How is this relevant to AI agents, model governance, data, copyright, security, enterprise adoption, or AI infrastructure?

Do not rely only on media interpretation for policy claims. Use media only to explain market reaction or provide secondary context after official sources have been checked.
